dm hint bar · v2

B chosen: key caps and pipes, with the pipe made faint. One component for every shortcut row.

What changed from v1. Style B is now the only style: a filled cap around each key, the label after it, a pipe between hints. The pipe is a derived faint tone (#4b4d5a, 45% from rail background toward rail dim), not rail dim itself. The caps carry the hover ground, so a full-row band is gone: the row ground stays rail background and the caps are the common background. Border placement still saves the row.

The bar

anatomy
 enter  pick │  esc  close │  j/k  move │  1-9  jump

Cap: one space of padding either side of the key, hover ground, key in rail text (bar accent on the dashboard where the key is the point). Label: one space after the cap, rail text. Pipe: one space either side, faint. Multi-key tokens stay whole: j/k, ctrl+u/ctrl+d.

The caps do the boxing the band used to do. The row is calmer, and the bar costs two more cells per hint.

Pipe tone

Four tones on the rail background. The chosen one is 45%. Fainter than 35% reads as a scratch on the terminal; brighter than 60% competes with the label.

Open questions

Note the element you mean, or send an empty review if this is the shape.
1. Border placement for the session picker and layout palette, where it saves a row? The picker loses its tee; the palette loses its footer line (its "preview dropped" note needs a new home).
2. Labels at rail text, as here, or one step dimmer so the caps lead harder?
3. The dashboard's row loses its full band and gains caps. Keep it that way, or keep the band there and make the caps a lighter derived ground?
4. The cap ground is rail hover. On the border row it reads as a small inset block; is that enough contrast, or should a border bar use the focus ground?
5. Ask float: the two that end the form survive at 44 cells. Is that enough, or does "hide" need to survive too?
